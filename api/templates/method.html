🧠 How this site helps you never forget

Most people study by re-reading and highlighting. It feels productive, but research shows it's one of the weakest ways to learn. This site is built on the methods that scientists found work best, and every feature uses one of them.

The 8 methods (and where they are on this site)

1. 🧠 Retrieval practice

Pulling an answer out of your memory strengthens it far more than reading it again. Struggling a little is good: it's a "desirable difficulty".

On this site: recall boxes after every lesson part, flashcards, quizzes.

2. 📅 Spaced repetition

Review just as you're about to forget – after 1 day, then ~6 days, then weeks. Each review makes the memory last longer.

On this site: the Review page schedules every card with the SM-2 algorithm (the one Anki made famous). Do it daily – 10 minutes.

3. 🔀 Interleaving

Mix different topics in one session instead of doing one topic in a block. It's harder, but it teaches you to choose the right idea, which is what interviews test.

On this site: Mixed practice, and Review shuffles all topics together.

4. 🤔 Guess first (pretesting)

Trying to answer questions before learning, even when you get them wrong, makes the lesson stick better afterwards.

On this site: the warm-up guess at the start of each guided lesson.

5. 🖼️ Dual coding

Words + pictures create two memory paths instead of one.

On this site: the animated diagrams (🎬) in lessons. Try drawing them yourself from memory too!

6. 🍎 Concrete examples & elaboration

Abstract ideas stick when tied to real examples and to what you already know. Ask yourself "why?" and "how does this connect to my job?"

On this site: every lesson uses real testing examples; the "Explain it simply" step asks for an example from your own work.

7. 🧒 Feynman technique

Explain the topic in simple words as if teaching a friend. Where you get stuck shows exactly what you haven't understood yet.

On this site: the last step of every guided lesson; saved to your Notes.

8. 🍅 Focus + rest (chunking & Pomodoro)

Learn in small chunks with full focus (~25 min), then take a real break. Your brain keeps connecting ideas while you rest, and sleep locks memories in.

On this site: lessons split into parts, and the 🍅 timer in guided lessons.

Your daily routine (1 hour)

  1. Review due cards first (10 min) – grade yourself honestly.
  2. One guided lesson (25 min, one 🍅) – guess, read a part, recall, compare.
  3. 5-minute break – walk, water, no phone.
  4. Mixed practice or a quiz (10 min).
  5. Explain or write a card (5 min) – in your own words.
  6. Sleep well – that's when memory is stored.

Traps to avoid

  • ❌ Re-reading notes many times: it feels familiar, but familiar ≠ remembered. Test yourself instead.
  • ❌ Cramming everything on Sunday: spread it out – a little every day beats a lot once.
  • ❌ Watching videos without doing: always type the code yourself in the Console.
  • ❌ Skipping reviews: if you miss a day, just do the due cards the next day – don't start over.

📚 The books & research behind this

  • Make It Stick: The Science of Successful Learning – Brown, Roediger & McDaniel (2014). The best single book on this. Key ideas: retrieval practice, spacing, interleaving, desirable difficulties.
  • A Mind for Numbers and the free course Learning How to Learn – Barbara Oakley. Focused vs diffuse thinking, chunking, Pomodoro, recall. Coursera (free)
  • Dunlosky et al. (2013), "Improving Students' Learning With Effective Learning Techniques" – a big review of 10 study techniques. Practice testing and spaced practice were rated the most useful; highlighting and re-reading were rated low.
  • The Learning Scientists – six strategies: spaced practice, retrieval practice, elaboration, interleaving, concrete examples, dual coding. Free posters & guides
  • Pretesting research (e.g. Richland, Kornell & Kao, 2009) – guessing before learning improves later memory, even when the guess is wrong.
  • SM-2 algorithm – Piotr Woźniak (SuperMemo, 1987), the scheduling method used by the Review page.
